South Shore flood zones: what buyers should check

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson, Keller Williams Greater Nassau. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Before you bid on a South Shore home, look up the exact address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, read the seller's flood answers on the New York property condition disclosure, ask for any elevation certificate, and get a flood insurance quote. Since March 20, 2024, New York sellers must answer those flood questions.

What changed in New York in 2024?

Since March 20, 2024, sellers can no longer give a $500 credit at closing instead of filling out the property condition disclosure. The form now asks whether:

  • any of the property is in a FEMA-designated floodplain
  • it is in a special flood hazard area (100-year floodplain) or a moderate flood hazard area (500-year floodplain)
  • the property is subject to a flood insurance requirement
  • the seller received FEMA, SBA or other federal disaster aid for flood damage
  • flood insurance is in place, and whether a FEMA elevation certificate exists
  • the seller has filed a flood insurance claim on the property

How do you check a house's flood zone?

  1. Search the address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.
  2. Compare what you find with the seller's disclosure answers.
  3. Ask for the elevation certificate, past flood insurance bills and any claims history.
  4. Get a flood insurance quote before you sign the contract, not after.
  5. Ask your home inspector to look for signs of past water in the basement, crawl space and garage.

Which South Shore towns have flood history?

Hurricane Sandy completely flooded the southern part of Oceanside in 2012. Freeport is a canal village, and Baldwin sits between two creeks. Lynbrook, Valley Stream and Rockville Centre do not front the bay, but FEMA maps risk lot by lot, so check every address.

What else do people ask?

Can a New York seller skip the flood disclosure?

No. Since March 20, 2024, the $500 credit that let sellers skip the property condition disclosure is gone, and the form includes flood questions.

What is the difference between a 100-year and a 500-year floodplain?

FEMA's special flood hazard area, often called the 100-year floodplain, has a 1 percent chance of flooding in any year. The moderate flood hazard area, often called the 500-year floodplain, has a 0.2 percent chance in any year.

How do I find the flood zone for a house?

Search the exact address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov. Then compare it with the seller's answers on the property condition disclosure.

What is an elevation certificate?

A document prepared by a licensed surveyor or engineer that records a building's elevation compared with expected flood levels. Insurers can use it to rate flood insurance, and the New York disclosure form asks whether one exists.
